Lt. Col. Jared Duffy, chief of staff to State Police Superintendent Steven James, remains suspended without pay. The FBI has taken over the investigation. No charges have been filed, and Duffy has not been arrested. That is where we left things in Episode 45. New information comes from Albany Times Union reporter Brendan Lyons, speaking with WAMC's Lucas Willard.

Lyons reported that the investigation originated with a tip from the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children. According to Lyons, the tip concerned sexually explicit images involving minors. Lyons reported that Duffy was questioned last week and underwent a polygraph examination at the Schodack Barracks in Rensselaer County. Lyons said the location appeared to have been chosen to keep the matter out of view. The barracks was cleared of personnel not involved in the questioning, and that clearing drew attention instead.

Lyons also reported that devices were removed from Duffy's home on October 20, and that the FBI now appears to be conducting forensic analysis. On that forensic work, Lyons offered his own assessment: an initial scan likely found nothing, because an arrest would probably have followed if it had. That is Lyons's read, not a finding from law enforcement.

Duffy's brother spoke with Lyons about the seized laptop. The brother told Lyons the laptop was not hidden. It was sitting in a junk drawer. The brother described a 2009 X account registered to a Yahoo email address that Duffy lost control of in September, apparently after it was hacked. After that loss of access, the long-unused account was alleged to have circulated links or information pointing to sexually explicit images of children, retweeted by accounts that appeared fabricated or hacked and were later shut down. The brother's question, as Lyons relays it: why would a lieutenant colonel post material like that on an account bearing his own name and listing his hometown?

Lyons raised a procedural question as well: whether the State Police consulted the U.S. Attorney's office or the Albany County District Attorney before obtaining the search warrant. That question has not been answered publicly.

On what comes next, Lyons offered two possibilities. If evidence is found, a charge. If it is not, a public clearing of Duffy's name.

A note before we move on. Everything in this segment that touches on alleged conduct is attributed to Lyons's reporting or to the brother as Lyons relays him. Charges are accusations; they require proof. Here, no charge has even been filed. Jared Duffy is presumed innocent.

New York State Police have confirmed that a 17-year-old passenger died after a dirt bike crash in Hurley, Ulster County. The crash happened around 6:30 in the evening on Friday, October 23. The confirmation came in reporting by the Democrat and Chronicle and others this week.

According to State Police, the crash occurred at the intersection of Schoolhouse Lane and Main Street in Hurley. An unregistered dirt bike was carrying an 18-year-old driver and a 17-year-old passenger, traveling west on Main Street. The driver attempted to pass a Hyundai that was turning left onto Schoolhouse Lane. The dirt bike and the Hyundai collided. Both people on the dirt bike were ejected.

Both were treated at the scene and taken to Kingston HealthAlliance Hospital. The 17-year-old passenger died at the hospital. The condition of the driver has not been released.

State Police have not identified the teenager who died, the dirt bike driver, or the Hyundai driver. The investigation is open. Anyone with information is asked to contact the State Police Kingston Bureau of Criminal Investigation at 845-344-5300.

The Millerton News reported Tuesday that the Dutchess County early education pilot program is moving toward design. The headline fits what the sourcing has shown for months: money and basic design are in place, and the work now is on how families actually reach the program.

To understand what moving toward design means, it helps to go back to the announcement. In January, Dutchess County was put on track, alongside Monroe and Broome counties, for $60 million in state funding for a community care pilot. Dutchess was set to receive $20 million, and the county committed an additional 10 percent match of $2 million. The program was designed to focus on children ages 0 to 3, with year-round full-day care available regardless of income, beginning in 2027.

That was the announcement. The harder question is what reaching rural northern Dutchess actually looks like.

On August 19, about 30 parents, employers, providers, community leaders, and elected officials gathered at the Pine Plains Free Library for a forum organized by County Legislator Chris Drago and the Child Care Council of Dutchess and Putnam. The forum was reported by the New Pine Plains Herald.

The pilot is intended to support as many as 1,000 child care seats in each county, with the first seats expected to come online in 2027. In the Pine Plains Central School District, there are just two registered family day cares for roughly 375 students across the district's two local schools. That gap reflects a wider one: Dutchess County has more than three children under 5 for each available childcare spot.

Participants at the August forum discussed four areas where the pilot could gain ground: helping home-based caregivers become registered; supporting existing programs that could expand; improving transportation; and using schools, churches, and other community buildings already in place.

Specific enrollment dates, site locations, or administrators have not been published in the sources read for this program. The first seats are a 2027 story. The design work is happening now.
