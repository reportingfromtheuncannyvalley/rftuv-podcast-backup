Starting today and running through Halloween, New York State Police and local law enforcement are stepping up patrols across the state targeting impaired and reckless drivers. Governor Kathy Hochul announced the campaign Saturday. It runs from today, Sunday, October twenty fifth, through this coming Saturday, October thirty first, and it is funded by the Governor's Traffic Safety Committee. Officers will be deployed in greater numbers, sobriety checkpoints will be established at locations across the state, and there will be particular attention paid to underage drinking. Drivers in Dutchess County and across New York should expect a heavier law enforcement presence on the roads every day through the end of the month.

The numbers from last year's effort give a clear sense of the scale and the seriousness of the initiative. State figures from the prior Halloween campaign show forty four thousand five hundred forty five citations issued statewide, including one thousand two hundred ten for impaired driving and eight thousand seven hundred one for speeding. Those figures come from the Governor's release announcing this year's campaign, and they reflect what an enforcement week at this level actually produces across New York.

Governor Hochul put it plainly in her statement Saturday. She said everyone should be able to enjoy Halloween and make great memories, but too many drivers make poor decisions with deadly consequences. She said there will be no tolerance for impaired, distracted, or speeding drivers.

DMV Acting Commissioner Christian Jackstadt spoke to the practical question people will have as the weekend's celebrations get underway. If a celebration includes alcohol or drugs, he said, plan a sober ride. That is the guidance, stated simply, and it applies to every event between now and Saturday night.

Today is also the first full day of early voting and the last full weekend before Halloween. If you are getting behind the wheel at any point this week, the enforcement picture is meaningfully different from what it was a week ago. Plan accordingly.

Patch reported on October twenty third, in a story by Michelle Tuccitto Sullo, that on Wednesday, October twenty first, two Dutchess County District Attorney's office crime analysts, Heather Palazzo and Kristina Crespo, discovered a woman overdosing in a parking deck stairwell in Poughkeepsie. Palazzo began assessing the woman's vital signs and administering life-saving aid while Crespo called nine hundred eleven and notified investigators in the DA's office. Members of the office's Investigations Unit rushed to the scene with Narcan, and Investigator Kristoffer Adams administered two doses. The staff helped save the woman's life. Patch does not report the woman's name, age, hospital, or further condition, and the desk is not adding detail beyond what Patch established. District Attorney Anthony Parisi said Thursday he was immensely proud of everyone involved. He credited Palazzo and Crespo's courage and presence of mind and the Investigations Unit's swift response. Parisi had directed all staff to receive Narcan training. Staff completed mandatory training in May twenty twenty four through the Dutchess County Department of Behavioral and Community Health, and Narcan was made available to every office member and on every floor of the building. The office said the actions on October twenty first showed how preparation, access to life-saving medication, and quick response can make a critical difference.
