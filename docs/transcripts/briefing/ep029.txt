Noel: Story One: Anthropic turns to lawyers to help A I agents strike deals

Noel: Anthropic is consulting legal professionals on how A I agents should negotiate to secure the best deals for the people using them. The Financial Times reported this Sunday, citing two people familiar with Anthropic's thinking, that the company is seeking advice from law firms on how lawyers represent clients' interests while ensuring fair negotiation outcomes.

Noel: This is a different question from the one this program covered yesterday, when Anthropic was asking lawmakers for safety rules. Anthropic is asking the profession that writes and negotiates deals how an agent should behave inside one.

Noel: The company has been studying what negotiation norms A I models should follow when interacting with one another. As personal agents become more capable, companies are thinking about a future in which agents communicate with one another and negotiate on users' behalf - and where those agents will encounter other agents whose goals conflict with theirs.

Noel: The open questions, as the reporting frames them. How much should an agent check back with its user during a negotiation? How wary should it be of other A I agents, without tipping into what the Financial Times called conspiracy-style thinking? And, from the India Today summary of the same story, the tasks in view include negotiating deals, resolving complaints, and securing better salary offers - all while following legal and ethical rules. The reporting raises how much freedom agents should be given as a separate question that remains unsettled.

Noel: Why lawyers specifically? A lawyer's job in a negotiation is to represent a client's interests inside rules: what may be offered, what must be disclosed, where the client's authority ends, and what a fair outcome looks like when the other side has interests too. The problem Anthropic appears to be studying is a translation problem - what a lawyer knows about representing a client that a prompt does not already contain, and who is liable for a deal an agent strikes on someone's behalf.

Noel: Anthropic has not commented on the record. Watch whether the company publishes any negotiation norms, whether any law firm or rival lab responds publicly, and whether any agent-negotiated deal is ever described in public with its terms and its liability attached.

Noel: Story Two: N Y-seventeen - Conley makes her case against Lawler, and both campaigns court the Jewish vote

Noel: New York's seventeenth Congressional District runs through the lower Hudson Valley. Republican Representative Mike Lawler, the second-term incumbent, is facing Democratic challenger Cait Conley. The Cook Political Report and the University of Virginia Center for Politics have both rated the race a toss-up. Election Day is Tuesday, November third - sixteen days out. Early voting opens Saturday, October twenty fourth.

Noel: Recent polling reflects how close the race is. An Emerson College and PIX11 News survey of four hundred likely voters, conducted September twenty seventh to twenty nine, had Conley ahead forty eight to forty six. A poll paid for by the Lawler campaign, conducted September twenty eighth to thirty, had Lawler ahead fifty to forty seven. Both leads fell within the four point nine percent margins of error in their respective surveys, per The Journal News.

Noel: The challenger's case, in her campaign's terms. Cait Conley is a fourth-generation Hudson Valley native and an Army combat veteran. In the final televised debate on PIX11 on Wednesday, October fourteenth, she opened with her sixteen-year Army career and deployments to Middle East combat zones. Her stated case is that Lawler votes with Donald Trump one hundred percent of the time in a measure she cites - even when it harms his own constituents - while she is running to rein in Washington corruption, lower middle-class taxes, and hold monopoly utility companies and Big Pharma accountable. On the cost of living, she said at the debate that high prices at gas pumps and grocery stores are results of the Iran war and tariffs, actions President Trump took and Lawler supported, and that voters are stuck literally paying the price.

Noel: The incumbent's case, in his campaign's terms. Lawler opened that same debate with his bipartisan work in Congress and his legislative record. He pointed to last year's tax cuts and a housing package he said will increase the supply of new homes, and to a pending measure to speed permits for new energy production. On the Trump-alignment charge, he cites a figure putting his alignment at ninety four percent, and he listed instances in the debate where he broke with Trump - including on the cancellation of temporary protected status for Haitian immigrants, which he said puts his Haitian constituents in Rockland County at risk.

Noel: The constituency fight: more than twenty percent of the voters in N Y-seventeen are Jewish, per reporting checked for this edition. The Jewish Telegraphic Agency reports that both campaigns are competing directly for that vote. In the debates, both candidates expressed strong support for Israel and condemned antisemitism. Lawler called Israel "our greatest ally in the world" and said he opposes any effort to defund the strategic partnership between the United States and Israel. Conley said she believes in Israel's right to exist and America's responsibility to help support the safety and security of the people of Israel, and added she will criticize the Netanyahu administration when she believes it is doing the wrong thing for Israel and for America.

Noel: At the October fourteenth debate, when Lawler pressed Conley on New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's remarks around the October seventh anniversary, Conley said she does denounce Mamdani's statement, calling October seventh "a day that should be honoring and mourning the loss of life, the greatest loss of life to the Jewish community since the Holocaust," per The Journal News. Lawler has also highlighted endorsements from two local Democratic Jewish officials - New Castle Town Councilwoman Ally Chemtob and Montebello Deputy Mayor Stacy Caridi - who cited his efforts to fight antisemitism.

Noel: Watch for any new endorsements, statements, or ad moves from either campaign in the final two weeks, and turnout when early voting opens October twenty fourth.

Noel: Story Three: A phone built around an A I agent, not apps

Noel: On October thirteenth, Chinese A I startup StepFun launched a phone called the STEPX Neo. The phone's A I agent is built into the operating system itself rather than sitting in an app. It runs Step AOS one point zero and the agent is called Amoo. Pre-orders opened that evening on JD.com, Tmall, and Douyin at three thousand nine hundred ninety nine yuan - about five hundred sixty dollars - with a first sales event on October sixteenth. The price includes twelve months of A I access at one hundred billion tokens a month. StepFun has not said what the A I costs after the first year, per Decrypt, which reported the launch on October seventeenth.

Noel: StepFun's pitch, in the company's terms: instead of opening a ride-hailing app, typing an address, and picking a car, you tell the phone where you want to go and the agent handles the rest. The company told its launch audience that in the agent era, A I is no longer an add-on to a smartphone but the product itself. StepFun says Step AOS one point zero was built from scratch for the agent, with long-term memory, task scheduling, and security controls in the base layer, using a mix of Android, Linux, and real-time operating system components. The operating system breaks phone functions - messaging, apps, files, settings - into small blocks the agent can call directly, using Model Context Protocol, or MCP.

Noel: Users get two ways in: the familiar app grid, and a separate space for the agent. Amoo keeps one memory for the user's facts, habits, and preferences, and another for what it has learned about completing tasks. Simple commands like setting an alarm run on a small model inside the phone, some offline. Harder requests that span several apps go to a larger model in the cloud. A second MiniLED screen on the back shows what Amoo is doing.

Noel: Nearly one hundred companies have signed on, including Alipay, Tencent, Ctrip, Didi, and Meituan. Payments, travel bookings, and content creation are still completed by their own platforms. StepFun describes this as its alternative to the screen-reading and simulated-click approach ByteDance's Doubao phone agent used - an approach that drew blocks from major Chinese apps in December - per Decrypt. StepFun was founded in twenty twenty three by former Microsoft vice president Jiang Daxin and first showed the agent, the operating system, and the STEPX brand in Shanghai on July thirteenth, without a price or a sales date.

Noel: No availability outside China is established in the Decrypt reporting. Watch whether the phone ships and works as StepFun describes following the October sixteenth sales event, what the A I access costs after year one, and whether any maker outside China places the same bet.
