Noel: Story three - PFC Albert Marks, Kingston

Noel: Our local close tonight is quiet, and it matters.

Noel: United States Army Private First Class Albert H. Marks was from Kingston, New York. He served in Company G, second Battalion, three hundred fifty seventh Infantry Regiment, ninetyth Infantry Division. On December seventh, nineteen forty four, his unit took part in an assault to capture Dillingen, Germany. Near the town of Pachten, Marks was killed by enemy artillery. His body could not be recovered. His name was placed on the Walls of the Missing at Lorraine American Cemetery in Saint-Avold, France.

Noel: In nineteen forty six, remains buried in Reimsbach, Germany were exhumed. There was no identification connecting them to Marks at that time. Years later, a historian at the Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency made a connection between a set of those remains and Marks's record. That set was disinterred in June twenty twenty one. Laboratory scientists at the DPAA used modern forensic techniques to make a positive identification. The agency officially accounted for Private First Class Marks on April second, twenty twenty six.

Noel: On October ninth, twenty twenty six, Albany International Airport conducted a dignified transfer. His remains arrived aboard a Southwest Airlines flight. Army National Guard Honor Guard members carried out the transfer in the presence of family and friends.

Noel: He had been missing for more than eighty years. He is home.
