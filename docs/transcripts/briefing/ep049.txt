Six days from tonight, voters in New York's 17th Congressional District go to the polls. Tuesday brought the district's highest-profile visitors yet, and a dispute over a mailing that has no clear source.

House Speaker Mike Johnson, Republican of Louisiana, traveled to Orangeburg in Rockland County to campaign for Representative Mike Lawler, who is seeking a third term. Johnson estimated as much as 60 million dollars will be spent in the district and predicted Lawler will win. Three days earlier, House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries stumped for Democrat Cait Conley at an event in Peekskill. Both House party leaders have now made the trip in the final week. The Cook Political Report rates the race a toss-up.

The district takes in Rockland and Putnam counties, the part of Westchester north of White Plains, and three towns in southern Dutchess. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3.

The Lawler campaign says a mailing from a group calling itself the American Forge Coalition arrived in district homes on October 23 and 24. Lawler says the group has no Federal Election Commission record and that the mailing misstates his position, claiming he supports amnesty for undocumented immigrants convicted of crimes, which he says he does not. Lawler says his campaign contacted the U.S. Department of Justice, which referred the matter to the FBI. Campaign manager Ciro Riccardi has called on Conley to condemn the mailing. The reporting, by David McKay Wilson on Substack on October 27, notes the Conley campaign did not return calls and emails over two days. No source establishes who paid for the mailing.

On Tuesday evening, Conley held a town hall in Nanuet on the topic of dark money in politics. Tiffany Muller, president of End Citizens United, joined her.

The final debate in the race was October 14, with the sharpest exchanges over immigration enforcement, sanctuary policies, and bail reform. October polling reported by Emerson College and the New York Post showed Conley at 48 percent and Lawler at 46 percent, with about 7 percent undecided. That is October polling, not current.

Social media videos believed to be produced with artificial intelligence and linked to Russia are misusing real people's names and photographs to spread disinformation about two Democratic Senate candidates in the final stretch of midterm voting. That report, from the Associated Press, was published today by correspondent Ali Swenson.

In Ohio, a video depicting a newscast includes pictures of Haya Akbik, who interned for Senator Sherrod Brown several years ago, along with other former staff members. Akbik told the AP the claims attributed to her are, in her words, "completely made up," adding: "It's my name, it's my reputation, making this allegation, and it's just completely false." She said the video harms the senator in the process, and said it shows the importance of Congress regulating what she called AI slop on the internet, saying: "This is so damaging." Brown is running against Republican Jon Husted. The AP reports the video appears to include some AI manipulation.

In Texas, a video framed as a direct-to-camera statement from a former student of Democratic candidate James Talarico is also believed to include some AI manipulation. Talarico is running against Republican Ken Paxton. JT Ennis, a spokesperson for the Talarico campaign, said in a statement that the campaign is being slandered with AI deepfakes, foreign bots, and conspiracy theories.

Darren Linvill, a professor at Clemson University, says the videos resemble clips from the Russian network Storm-1516 that targeted Kamala Harris and Tim Walz in 2024 and are likely products of the same network. Hany Farid, a professor at Dartmouth College, says his analyses show both videos were likely generated or manipulated using AI. Farid called the Brown video a "fairly low-tech fake."

X has taken down the initial posts and suspended the accounts for violating its rules, but copies could still be found online on Tuesday, some with hundreds of shares. The Russian Embassy did not immediately respond to the AP and has denied interfering in past election cycles. The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency also did not respond.

A look ahead to Halloween, and then to the weekend as a whole.

As of this Wednesday forecast, Saturday, October 31 is expected to be mostly sunny in Poughkeepsie, with a high of 57 degrees and a 10 percent chance of rain. Sunset comes at 5:51 in the evening. Saturday night turns partly cloudy and cold, with a low of 29 degrees. A forecast three days out can still change, so it's worth checking again as the week closes.

Meteorologist Ben Noll's Hudson Valley outlook, posted October 25, has the valley's dry streak reaching two weeks if no rain falls through Halloween.

One safety note for the weekend: a statewide Halloween traffic safety campaign runs through Saturday, targeting impaired, distracted, and reckless driving. Last year's campaign produced 1,210 impaired driving citations statewide.

Then, early Sunday morning, daylight saving time ends. At 2 AM on November 1, clocks go back one hour to 1 AM, giving you an extra hour of sleep that night. By the new clock, Sunday's sunset in Poughkeepsie arrives at 4:50 in the afternoon. That earlier close to the day is the trade for the morning light.

Wednesday evening's briefing: Party leaders in the district and a disputed mailing in NY-17, six days out. Fake videos borrowing real names in two Senate races, with takedowns trailing the copies. And a dry, cold Halloween ahead in the valley, with the clocks falling back right behind it.
