Noel: The Dutchess County legislature has passed a resolution aimed at New York's rules on data centers. The resolution carries the number two thousand twenty six one sixty three. It is a memorialization, meaning the county formally urges state action rather than setting its own binding law. The problem is a gap in Governor Kathy Hochul's executive order, which paused state permits for data centers using fifty megawatts or more of power. Projects drawing between twenty and forty nine megawatts fall entirely outside that pause. The resolution urges the governor to sign the Responsible Data Center Development Act. The bill is Senate ten thousand six hundred forty two and Assembly eleven thousand five hundred sixty, sponsored by Assemblymember Didi Barrett. The legislature passed the bill on June fourth. If signed, it would extend the pause to cover projects at twenty megawatts. It would also set standards on energy and water use, utility costs, public participation, labor protections, and benefits for host communities. The governor's office says she will review the bill. The resolution also calls on Dutchess County towns, cities, and villages to adopt temporary moratoriums for projects at twenty megawatts or more. The county planning department will draft model language those local governments can use as a starting point. Twenty one legislators signed on. Consider the case of East Fishkill, which adopted a three year moratorium running through July twenty twenty nine. The town acted after Donovan Drive Holdings proposed a one thousand megawatt project at the Wood Street three hundred forty five kilovolt substation, carrying interconnection queue number one thousand seven hundred thirty eight. On November sixteenth, the New York Independent System Operator filed new Large Load Integration Procedures with federal regulators. Under current rules, a thirty megawatt project could proceed with no state pause at all. Until Albany acts, the fight over new data center development sits with town boards. R F T U V's Mara Quinn reported this story; you can read it at rftuv.ai, published November twenty first.

Noel: Yesterday, the Regional Food Bank held a record Thanksgiving distribution across the Hudson Valley. The organization delivered two hundred thousand meals to more than fifty thousand people in six counties: Orange, Dutchess, Ulster, Sullivan, Rockland, and Putnam. That was roughly double the previous year's Hudson Valley effort. About a dozen sites handled the distribution. Each family received approximately forty two pounds: turkey, ham, stuffing, gravy mix, green beans, apples, potatoes, and apple pie. More than eighteen hundred volunteers turned out to help, including one hundred twenty at the Cornerstone Family Healthcare site in Newburgh. In Poughkeepsie, the distribution at Dutchess Community College ran drive through on a first come, first served basis. The state's Nourish New York program provided part of the funding. Bill Fioravanti, the food bank's Hudson Valley executive vice president, explained the goal this way: "We just wanted to come out and make Thanksgiving a meaningful experience for everyone, no matter their financial situation." The effort extends into this week. On Monday and Tuesday, November twenty third and twenty fourth, culinary students from Orange Ulster BOCES will cook one thousand prepared Thanksgiving meals for area seniors. Also this weekend, the thirtieth annual Stuff the Bus food drive ran Friday through Sunday at Stop and Shop stores in Poughkeepsie, collecting nonperishable food for Catholic Charities pantries. This report comes from the Times Herald-Record.

Noel: The F B I is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, the bureau's criminal justice database. The database holds records on fugitives, missing persons, criminal histories, and stolen property. The F B I's Criminal Justice Information Services division runs it. CNN first reported the breach Friday night, citing two people briefed on the matter. The Associated Press confirmed the investigation Saturday through an anonymous source. Much remains unknown. The F B I has declined to comment publicly. The scope of the intrusion is not established. What was accessed or taken, if anything, has not been confirmed. There is no public attribution and no arrests. President Trump addressed the matter Saturday, saying: "It's not good. I don't like that." On Sunday morning, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said he was not in a position to give details about the origin or whether anything was compromised, adding that the F B I is looking into it. A point of clarification: this is separate from a September breach of an F B I jobs portal claimed by the group known as ShinyHunters, which led to an arrest in October. The two incidents should not be conflated.

Noel: New York has opened applications for two student loan forgiveness programs through the Higher Education Services Corporation. The Nursing Faculty Loan Forgiveness Program offers up to eight thousand dollars a year for up to five years, for licensed registered nurses with a master's or doctorate who teach nursing at least twelve credit hours a year. The Young Farmers Loan Forgiveness Program offers up to ten thousand dollars a year for up to five years, for graduates of approved New York colleges in their first ten years of full time farming in the state. Combined funding is two point one million dollars, applications close December sixteenth, and Ellis Ward has a full how to on both programs at rftuv.ai.
