In both New York and Connecticut, cost of living leads what likely voters say matters most in their governor's race, and in both states the voters who name it are backing the Democratic incumbents. Quinnipiac University documented this in polls released this month.

In New York, Quinnipiac surveyed one thousand one hundred sixty seven likely voters from October fifteenth to October nineteenth, with a margin of error of plus or minus three point eight percentage points including the design effect. The poll was released October twenty first. From a list of ten issues, cost of living ranked first at twenty two percent, the economy second at nineteen percent, and immigration third at thirteen percent. In the full race, Hochul receives fifty five percent and Blakeman forty one percent. Among voters who named cost of living as their top issue, Hochul leads Blakeman seventy one to twenty four percent. Among those who named the economy, Hochul leads seventy two to twenty seven.

In Connecticut, Quinnipiac surveyed one thousand two hundred twenty five likely voters from October eighth to October twelfth, with a margin of error of plus or minus three point eight percentage points including the design effect. From a list of nine issues, cost of living ranked first at twenty four percent, the economy second at twenty one percent, and taxes third at thirteen percent. In the full race, Lamont receives fifty eight percent and Fazio thirty eight percent. Among Connecticut voters who named cost of living as most important, Lamont leads Fazio sixty seven to thirty. Among those who named the economy, Lamont leads seventy three to twenty five.
