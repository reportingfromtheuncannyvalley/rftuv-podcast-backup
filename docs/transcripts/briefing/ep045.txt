Three stories tonight. The State Police chief of staff is suspended without pay while the F B I is asked to take the case. Abuse survivors unanimously approve an eight-hundred-million-dollar settlement with the Archdiocese of New York. And in the eighteenth Congressional District, Pat Ryan and Jackie Auringer face each other in person for the first time, one week before Election Day.

We start in Albany.

On Sunday, October twenty fifth, the State Police announced that Lieutenant Colonel Jared Duffy, chief of staff for Superintendent Steven James, has been suspended without pay. The suspension follows a search of Duffy's home in Colonie on Tuesday, October twentyth, as part of an ongoing investigation. Duffy was also reportedly taken into custody and interviewed by State Police, according to WAMC.

State Police have asked the F B I's Albany field office to take over the case. The State Police statement said the agency had asked F B I Albany to adopt the case and directed further inquiries to the F B I. The F B I confirmed the request and said it would not comment on an ongoing investigation. State Police have not publicly disclosed the reason for the suspension.

The Albany Times Union previously reported, citing multiple police sources, that the investigation centered on sexually explicit images of children. That is a report about the focus of an investigation, not a charge. As of Sunday afternoon, no charges had been filed against Duffy. Anyone not charged is presumed innocent, and an administrative suspension is not a conviction or a finding of guilt.

The Democrat and Chronicle, reporting from the Times Union, added details on Monday. The investigation is said to have begun with a tip to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children. Duffy passed a polygraph examination last week, per the Times Union. According to that reporting, his brother, a Buffalo-based lawyer, said it appeared that Duffy's email account and a dormant X account had recently been hacked and could be connected to the investigation. State Police have not publicly confirmed those details.

Duffy joined the State Police in twenty two and has served as chief of staff since October twenty twenty four. As of this evening, no charges have been filed and no new statement has been issued by either agency.

Our second story.

On Saturday, October twenty fourth, attorneys announced that all claimants had unanimously agreed to an eight-hundred-million-dollar settlement with the Archdiocese of New York, resolving claims by approximately one thousand three hundred survivors of clergy sexual abuse, per Reuters. Jeff Anderson, whose firm represents three hundred of those survivors, said: Through their courage, sacrifice, and determination, survivors have helped bring about a reckoning and a meaningful step toward accountability.

The settlement closes more than seven years of litigation under New York's Child Victims Act, which opened a window for past claims. At eight hundred million dollars, it is second only to the eight-hundred-and-eighty-million-dollar Archdiocese of Los Angeles deal in twenty twenty four, per Reuters.

Beyond the money, the archdiocese agreed to release documents pertaining to sexual offenders, per Reuters. USA Today adds that the agreement calls for the public identification of credibly accused clergy and the release of offender files that have remained under seal. Survivors may also litigate against Church insurers for additional payouts, and the settlement avoids an archdiocese bankruptcy.

The deal has not yet been signed. Archbishop Ronald Hicks told priests an agreement had been reached but had not yet been signed, per Reuters. Final signing is expected the week of November sixth, per USA Today and CNN. The settlement is agreed but not final, and no payments have begun.

Now to the congressional race in our district.

New York's eighteenth District covers all of Orange County and parts of Ulster and Dutchess, including Beacon. Democratic incumbent Pat Ryan is seeking a third term. His Republican challenger is Jackie Auringer, twenty nine, a Kingston native who has never held elected office and works for her family's construction company, Urban Precast, per the Highlands Current.

The two candidates met in person for the first time on Thursday night, October twenty second, at Mount Saint Mary College in Newburgh, per Mid Hudson News. A week earlier, a Daily Freeman debate on October sixteenth over Zoom ended after Auringer read an opening statement and signed off; Ryan answered questions for about an hour, per the Highlands Current.

Both campaigns center on affordability. Ryan told the Highlands Current: Priority one, two, and three are lowering costs. He points to a bipartisan housing supply bill that became law in July, federal infrastructure funding, and legislation to study grocery price drivers and limit utility profits. Auringer, per her website and campaign materials as described by the Highlands Current, argues rising housing, energy, and other costs make it hard for working families to stay in the Hudson Valley. She calls for lower taxes, less government spending, and fewer regulations she says drive up housing and business costs. Her May candidacy line, as the Highlands Current quotes it: In construction, the work either holds or it does not. Washington has forgotten that. Auringer has portrayed Ryan as a Washington insider who has lost touch with the district. Ryan says his job is to fight back against harm from the Trump administration and to work constructively to move good things forward.

No recent independent public polling of the race has been released, per the Highlands Current. As of September thirtyth, Auringer had raised one hundred fifty three thousand dollars during the cycle compared to Ryan's five point three million, per the Highlands Current. Ryan won the seat in twenty twenty two by narrow margins and won re-election in twenty twenty four by fourteen points. Vassar professor Richard Born, quoted in the Highlands Current, calls Auringer's candidacy a long shot and says an upset would be the biggest in the country. That is Born's assessment.

Early voting is underway. Election Day in this race is November third.
