New York State Police have charged two Hudson men with attempted murder in connection with a shooting on October 20 in the Columbia County town of Stockport. That is according to a Troop K press release dated October 29.

Devin Mackey, 23, and Demetries Mackey, 21, both of Hudson, were arrested on October 26. The Bureau of Criminal Investigation at Livingston conducted the investigation with the Columbia County District Attorney's Office.

On October 20, at about 4:21 in the afternoon, State Police at Livingston responded to Hill Street in Stockport for a 44-year-old man with a gunshot wound. He was taken to a local medical facility with non-life-threatening injuries. He was not identified in the State Police release, and he is not named here.

Each defendant faces the same six charges: attempted murder in the second degree, burglary in the first degree, criminal possession of a weapon in the second degree, reckless endangerment in the first degree, assault in the second degree, and two counts of endangering the welfare of a child.

Both men were arraigned in Town of Stockport Court and remanded to the Columbia County Jail. A preliminary hearing is scheduled for today, Friday, October 30, at 11:00 a.m. in Town of Stockport Court. No outcome from that hearing is known at production time, and none is stated here.

No motive has been stated by any source, and none is offered here.

These are charges, not an outcome. Devin Mackey and Demetries Mackey are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

In Dutchess County, a federal judge has ordered the Town of Beekman to allow a sober home for women in recovery from drug or alcohol addiction to open.

U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald announced a preliminary injunction on consent, signed by U.S. District Judge Jessica G.L. Clarke. The injunction requires Beekman to allow the opening of the home.

The background comes from the government's complaint, filed March 5, 2026, in White Plains. According to the government, Beekman refused to allow a man named Patrick Potter to operate Bunkhouse, a sober living home intended for male veterans, first responders, and their family members. Those are the government's allegations, not findings.

After the complaint was filed, Potter submitted a third application, this one for a women's sober home at the same property, under the name Hudson Valley Collective. On October 16, 2026, Beekman's Planning Board approved that application and waived dozens of requirements.

Under the consent injunction, Beekman agreed to waive additional conditions so the home can open. It would begin with up to four residents, and could grow to eight, depending on state and county requirements. An on-site staff person is required.

Two tracks here. The home opening is settled by the injunction. The lawsuit itself remains pending. That includes the United States' claim that Beekman's alternate care facility classification lacked factual basis, and the government's claims for money damages. Those are contentions the government has made in court; they have not been decided.

No opening date was announced in the sources reviewed for this segment, and none is stated here.

General Motors will pay $220 million to settle allegations that it illegally sold drivers' private behavior and location data to data brokers, who sold it to insurance companies that could then change premiums.

The settlement was announced by a coalition of 36 attorneys general, including New York Attorney General Letitia James. New York will receive more than $12 million of the total; the exact figure is $12,054,341.

From 2016 to 2024, GM enrolled millions of drivers in its Smart Driver program. That program tracked how often drivers were on the road, when trips started and ended, instances of hard braking and hard acceleration, and speeding. The attorneys general alleged that data was sold without the knowledge or meaningful consent of drivers, and that the data brokers resold it to insurers who used it to adjust premiums.

GM discontinued Smart Driver in 2024, following reporting by The New York Times.

Under the settlement, GM must obtain affirmative express consent before collecting or disclosing driving and location data. It must also limit the data it collects and submit to both internal and external privacy program assessments.

GM responded with one statement. The company said the settlement addresses Smart Driver, a product it ended in 2024, and reinforces steps it has taken to strengthen its privacy practices.

This is a settlement of allegations. No finding of wrongdoing has been made by a court.
