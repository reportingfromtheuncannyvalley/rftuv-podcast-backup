Noel: On October sixteenth, twenty twenty six, SUNY New Paltz dedicated a permanent statue honoring Sojourner Truth. The work is titled "First Step to Freedom, eighteen twenty six." Sculptor Trina Greene, an Ulster County artist based in New Paltz, created it. The statue depicts Truth with her daughter Sophia and stands outside the Sojourner Truth Library, which has carried her name since nineteen seventy one.

Noel: The title names a real moment. In eighteen twenty six, the woman then known as Isabella walked out of Ulster County with her infant daughter Sophia. She had been born enslaved in Esopus, in this same county, and that walk was her first step toward freedom and toward the name the world would know.

Noel: Getting it there took five years. The installation was planned for twenty twenty one. The university's Black Studies Department publicly protested that it had been excluded from the commissioning process. Then-President Donald P. Christian postponed it indefinitely. The statue toured the region, stopping at Kingston City Hall in twenty twenty four, Ulster County offices, and the Newburgh Free Library, before returning to campus this year.

Noel: A firsthand account of the October sixteenth dedication, by Anthony S. Dandridge, notes that no Black Studies faculty attended and argues the dedication proceeded without acknowledging the earlier resistance. That is his observation. The statue is permanently home. How it got there is part of its history.

Noel: Black Market Canna Co., at eighty nine Main Street in Poughkeepsie, is closed to cannabis sales. The New York State Office of Cannabis Management posted a suspension sign on the business October fourteenth, citing an investigation into activities that, in its words, created a risk to public safety. Employees may access the building but cannot conduct any licensed activity. No seal order was issued. This is an allegation-stage action, not a final ruling.

Noel: Black Market was Dutchess County's first licensed dispensary in the city. It opened in late March twenty twenty four, held its grand opening on April twentyth, twenty twenty four, and had become part of the downtown business district.

Noel: The cases, carried as charges and allegations only. Owner Ali Lodhi was charged by the F B I in July with possession of a firearm after a felony conviction. A grand jury indictment followed September twenty first in the Southern District of New York. Judge Vincent L. Briccetti denied bail October fourteenth. Lodhi remains detained. Federal prosecutors have alleged multiple murder-for-hire schemes targeting his business partner. These are allegations. Lodhi has not been convicted.

Noel: Co-owner Pedro Baggett, who holds a forty nine percent interest per his attorney, has alleged in Dutchess County court that Lodhi mismanaged five million one hundred seventy seven thousand sixteen dollars in company funds, with claims of theft, embezzlement, self-dealing, and falsified records. Lodhi has counter-alleged that Baggett misappropriated funds and threatened him. No ruling has been issued. Baggett told the Poughkeepsie Journal he plans to open a new dispensary across town in November.

Noel: The suspension stands. No purchases at eighty nine Main Street. This is not a verdict.
