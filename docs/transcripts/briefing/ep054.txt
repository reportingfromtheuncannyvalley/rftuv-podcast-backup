Now to Sullivan County, where a judge has found the county in contempt, but the residents an injunction was intended to protect have already left.

WAMC and Radio Catskill report that Ulster County Judge Kevin Bryant found Sullivan County in contempt of an injunction intended to prevent residents from being removed from the Adult Care Center at Sunset Lake.

The center was home to ninety-three senior residents in August. According to court documents cited by WAMC, no residents remain.

The judge said the evidence showed county officials intentionally took numerous actions to transfer residents out. He rejected the county's argument that residents had left voluntarily.

WAMC reports that the decision says officials took steps to keep information about the injunction and temporary restraining order from residents. It also says law enforcement was used to prevent routine visits.

Radio Catskill, carrying River Reporter reporting, says the restraining order was issued September sixteenth and paused the closure process. The judge found the county willfully and knowingly interfered with it.

The reporting describes the judge's criticism of testimony from county officials, including County Manager Joshua Potosek. Sullivan County declined to comment, according to Radio Catskill.

Sanctions and penalties will be addressed in court on November twenty-fourth. This contempt ruling is separate from the ongoing legal challenge to the closure itself.

That distinction matters. The court has found that its order was violated. What follows from that finding is still to be decided.

And with the center empty, the question is painfully practical: what remedy is left for the people the order was supposed to keep there? A penalty against the county and an answer for those residents are not necessarily the same thing.

Our third story is a Halloween story without an alien announcement.

According to a post published yesterday by NVIDIA, Breakthrough Listen and the SETI Institute are bringing agentic artificial intelligence into the daily search for extraterrestrial intelligence at the Allen Telescope Array in Northern California.

Here, agents means AI systems that can carry out a sequence of tasks. From a single request in ordinary language, they can steer the instrument, enhance and correct incoming data, and apply AI models to the live stream.

The system can handle two point four terabits per second, processed on site, next to the instrument, using NVIDIA's Holoscan platform.

And here's what it has demonstrated: the pipeline achieved the first real-time AI detection of a pulsar in the Crab Nebula, a known natural source. No extraterrestrial signal is claimed.

A pulsar is a rapidly spinning stellar remnant. In this case, hearing it was proof of the pipeline, not evidence of alien technology.

Andrew Siemion of the SETI Institute, who leads Breakthrough Listen, calls the development a breakthrough in translation, according to NVIDIA's post.

That's a useful way to understand the work: turning a human request into coordinated actions at an instrument. The search is for something unknown. The first demonstration depended on hearing something known.
