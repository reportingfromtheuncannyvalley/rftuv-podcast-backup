Noel: Our third story tonight comes out of Washington. On Thursday, the Treasury Department proposed a new rule that would expand restrictions on American high-technology investment in China and a set of other countries that Treasury classifies as adversaries.

Noel: The underlying program is the Outbound Investment Security Program. It launched in twenty twenty three with a focus on three sectors: semiconductors, quantum computing, and artificial intelligence. Congress codified it into law in December of last year. Thursday's proposal would widen the net in two directions at once.

Noel: First, on countries. The current rules center on China. The proposed expansion would add Cuba, Iran, North Korea, and Russia.

Noel: Second, on sectors. The new proposal adds hypersonics, supercomputing, biotechnology, and a category Treasury is calling superintelligence within A I.

Noel: The structure of the restrictions varies by activity. Some investments would be banned outright. Others would trigger a notification requirement rather than a prohibition. Publicly traded stocks are exempt. Direct investments and joint ventures face the harder restrictions. Deals already in place would not be unwound.

Noel: One detail to flag for the life sciences and biotech space: Treasury's own fact sheet indicates that licensing arrangements are not off limits under the proposal. BioCentury reported Thursday that how a deal is structured will continue to matter. This means the line between what is allowed and what is not may turn substantially on transaction mechanics.

Noel: Three voices on the proposal from reporting by AP, Barron's, and BioSpace, published Thursday and Friday.

Noel: John Crowley, the chief executive of the Biotechnology Innovation Organization, said this: details matter, and we cannot restrict our way to success.

Noel: Craig Singleton, a senior fellow at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, offered a different frame. Capital can accelerate military-relevant capabilities.

Noel: Christopher Pilkerton, the Treasury assistant secretary overseeing the program, framed it this way: the proposed regulations address risks that arise when foreign adversaries benefit from American investment in key technology sectors.

Noel: This is a proposed rule, not a final one. A public comment period follows. Nothing is in effect yet.
