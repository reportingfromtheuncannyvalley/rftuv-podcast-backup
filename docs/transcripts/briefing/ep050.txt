We start in Newburgh.

Newburgh city police have charged a Poughkeepsie man in connection with a gunpoint robbery reported October 22 in the City of Newburgh, according to the Times Herald-Record as carried in the RFTUV blotter.

The robbery was reported on City Terrace. Detectives assigned to the Non-Fatal Shooting Task Force canvassed the area. They located a person matching the description of the suspect riding a scooter near First Street and Carpenter Avenue. When detectives attempted to stop him near a residence on Grand Street, the man abandoned the scooter and ran. He fled on foot through several backyards and over multiple fences before police took him into custody near 114 First Street.

Police identified the suspect as Carlos Diggs, 38, of Poughkeepsie. He is charged with first-degree robbery and second-degree criminal possession of a weapon, both felonies. A detective recovered a 9-millimeter semi-automatic handgun at the scene.

Court information and the name of Diggs's attorney were not immediately available. These are charges, not a verdict. Carlos Diggs is presumed innocent until proven guilty.

Our second story is out of Orange County, where a husband and wife who operated Medicaid transportation companies were sentenced Tuesday for a fraud scheme that, according to a forensic audit by the Office of the State Comptroller, billed the state for trips that never happened and for group rides that were split on paper into separate fares.

Rohail Raja, 45, of New Windsor, owned Unique Class Limo Inc. On Tuesday, October 27, a judge in Orange County Court sentenced him to 6 to 18 years in prison. He had pleaded guilty to first-degree grand larceny.

His wife, Sharma Alam, 45, also of New Windsor, owned SNR Limo Inc. She was sentenced to six months incarceration and five years probation. She had pleaded guilty to fourth-degree conspiracy, a felony.

The Comptroller's forensic audit found that the state Health Department had paid both companies for transporting Medicaid recipients on dates and times when provider records show no such visits occurred. Auditors also found trips billed as separate transports that were in fact group trips, each passenger logged as a solo fare.

The scheme ran between April 15, 2020 and September 30, 2024. Over that period, the two companies received $3,434,730 more than they were entitled to collect from the state. At sentencing on Tuesday, Raja and Alam paid $400,000 toward restitution. They were ordered to pay the remaining $3,034,730.

The companies themselves were also charged. On May 14, 2026, Unique Class Limo Inc. pleaded guilty to first-degree grand larceny and admitted stealing $2,666,634. It was ordered to pay restitution in that amount and was fined $5,000. SNR Limo Inc. pleaded guilty the same day to second-degree grand larceny and admitted stealing $768,096. It was ordered to pay restitution in that amount and was also fined $5,000. In each case, the restitution ordered matched exactly the amount the company admitted to taking.

Orange County District Attorney David Hoovler said it was the largest Medicaid fraud scheme ever prosecuted by his office.

Medical transportation is a service Medicaid patients in Orange County rely on to reach appointments and receive care. The audit's finding is that for more than four years, those billing records did not reflect what actually happened.

The investigation was conducted jointly by the Orange County District Attorney's Office, the Office of the State Comptroller, the Orange County Sheriff's Office, and the Orange County White Collar Crimes Taskforce, assisted by the State Office of the Medicaid Inspector General and the Hudson Valley Crime Analysis Center.

Our third story is national. Alphabet, the parent company of Google, reported third-quarter earnings after the close on Wednesday, October 28. Shares fell more than 10 percent in after-hours trading, wiping nearly $400 billion off the company's market value.

The top-line results were strong. Revenue grew 25 percent in the quarter, reaching $127.6 billion, just below what analysts had expected. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled. Measured by those numbers, the business is growing at a pace most companies do not reach.

The number that moved the stock was the spending side of the ledger. Capital expenditure for the quarter came to $51.4 billion. Free cash flow for the quarter was negative $9.07 billion. That is the second straight quarter in which the company spent more cash than its operations generated. The prior quarter's outflow was $5.9 billion, and that quarter was already notable because it was the first time in Alphabet's 22 years as a public company that free cash flow had gone negative at all.

Chief Financial Officer Anat Ashkenazi said on the earnings call that capital expenditure will increase significantly in 2027. The company's 2026 forecast, set between $195 billion and $205 billion, is unchanged.

Microsoft reported the same afternoon. It beat analyst forecasts and rose about 3 percent in after-hours trading.

Thursday's regular trading session is the first full market day since Alphabet's report. Amazon and Intel are both scheduled to release results later today, which makes this afternoon a significant test for anyone trying to read where the AI infrastructure trade is heading. The market is sitting with this question: Can the cash the business generates keep pace with the bill for building the infrastructure underneath it? Wednesday night's move shows investors are not yet sure it can.
