The third story is about a distinction the AP makes carefully: hands-free is not eyes-off.

The Associated Press reported on October twenty third, twenty twenty six, from Warren, Michigan, with authors Alexa St. John and Mike Householder. Since twenty seventeen, GM has sold vehicles with Super Cruise, a hands-free assisted driving system available on over six hundred thirty thousand roads in the United States and Canada.

GM says its eyes-off version will be possible by twenty twenty eight, launching the next Super Cruise on the Cadillac Escalade IQ, a luxury electric sport utility vehicle. Tyson Becker, GM executive director for autonomous vehicle strategy and product, said the technology is meant to be so safe that it allows a driver to take eyes off the road while active and do other things in the cabin, per AP. That is a twenty twenty eight target. It is not available to buyers today.

GM is testing it at the GM Global Technical Center in Warren, Michigan, where hundreds of volunteer drivers sit in real GM vehicles inside a three hundred sixty-degree immersive simulator. The vehicle is real. The world around it is not. Drivers face road conditions including hills, construction, rain, and nearby traffic, while GM measures eye movement, heart rate, and stress from a nearby control room.
