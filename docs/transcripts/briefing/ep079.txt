Noel: New York has enacted a law banning subscription fees for vehicle features that rely on hardware already installed when you bought the car. Governor Hochul signed it on Monday, November twenty third. The practice of charging monthly or annual fees to activate built-in functions had drawn complaints from buyers across the country, and New York moved to end it.

Noel: The law is specific. The prohibition binds manufacturers, dealers, and third-party service providers alike. If a feature uses components already in the vehicle at purchase, activates without ongoing cost to the manufacturer or dealer, and simply works once turned on, the company cannot charge a recurring fee for it. Heated seats are the clearest example. You paid for that hardware when you bought the car. Using it should not require an additional monthly payment.

Noel: The law includes defined exceptions. Navigation system updates, infotainment services, satellite radio, in-vehicle Wi-Fi, telematics, roadside assistance, and connected services running over cellular or other data networks may still charge subscription fees. Those involve infrastructure with genuine recurring costs. The law separates features that require a live service to function from features that are simply hardware waiting to be switched on.

Noel: Violations carry a civil penalty of up to two hundred fifty dollars per point of sale per violation. The law takes effect on February twenty seventh, twenty twenty seven.
