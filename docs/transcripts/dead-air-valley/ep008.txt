Silas: Southern Italian folk belief is a serious subject. It encompasses the understandings that people carried from the villages of Calabria, Sicily, Campania, and elsewhere, the ways of reading the world, the protective practices, the beliefs that ran alongside the church and sometimes predated it. The evil eye is perhaps the most widely recognized, the idea that envy or malice concentrated in a gaze can cause harm, and that harm can be countered by someone who knows how. That knowledge was transmitted person to person, grandmother to grandchild. It was not casual. It was practical. It was what you did when something needed to be done.

Silas: These beliefs did not stop traveling when the first generation of Italian immigrants arrived in America. They crossed the Atlantic with their carriers, adapted to new circumstances, and persisted across generations, in kitchens, in family stories, in the things grandmothers did and swore by and sometimes declined to explain in full.
